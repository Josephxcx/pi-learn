Where symptoms start
is a clue.

Nutrient mobility helps explain which tissue shows deficiency first. Position is one clue; it does not establish a diagnosis by itself.

The plant redistributes.

When a nutrient is mobile within the plant, older tissue can supply younger growth. Deficiency symptoms therefore often appear in older leaves first.

Relatively immobile nutrients cannot be readily redistributed, so young tissue or growing points are often affected first.

Compare the pattern.

1 Locate

Check whether older leaves, younger leaves, or the growing point are affected first.

2 Compare

Older-leaf symptoms suggest considering mobile nutrients. Young-tissue symptoms suggest considering less mobile nutrients, along with other possible causes.

3 Confirm

Combine the pattern with crop species, root health, soil conditions, and appropriate tissue or soil testing. Symptoms can overlap with diseases or environmental stress.
