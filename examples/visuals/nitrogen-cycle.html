Follow nitrogen.
Name the transformation.

Nitrogen changes chemical form. Each transformation has different organisms, conditions, and consequences.

1

Atmospheric N₂

Fixation converts nitrogen gas into ammonia, which forms ammonium in soil.

N₂ → NH₃ / NH₄⁺

2

Soil ammonium

Nitrification oxidises ammonium via nitrite to nitrate, usually under aerobic conditions.

NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻

3

Living tissue

Assimilation incorporates absorbed ammonium or nitrate into organic compounds.

NH₄⁺ / NO₃⁻ → organic N

4

Organic residues

Ammonification converts organic nitrogen in residues back into ammonium.

Organic N → NH₄⁺

A branch, not a single loop.

Denitrification reduces nitrate to gases such as N₂O and N₂, typically where oxygen is limited. Nitrate can also be lost through leaching. These routes differ from plant uptake.

1 Separate the forms

Atmospheric nitrogen gas, ammonium, nitrate, and organic nitrogen are different pools. Plants generally take up ammonium and nitrate; they do not directly assimilate atmospheric N₂.

2 Separate the processes

Fixation begins with N₂. Nitrification begins with NH₄⁺. Ammonification begins with organic nitrogen. Name the process by its starting and ending forms.

3 Connect conditions to losses

Oxygen-limited soil can favour denitrification. Nitrate is also mobile in soil water, so leaching is another loss pathway.
